Find Reasons to Compliment

A genuine compliment makes a guest feel seen. Not flattered. Seen. Look for something real and name it.

Example

That's a beautiful sweater, Mrs. Higgins.

Example

I love that bag. Great choice.

Example

Your kids are so well behaved. What a great little crew.

Example

Happy birthday, by the way. We're so glad you're spending it with us.

  • Something they're wearing, something they said, something about their group
  • Use their name if you have it. A name is the best compliment you can pay.
  • One genuine compliment lands better than five generic ones
  • Never force it. If it isn't real, don't say it.

Gratitude is not a script. It's a posture. Every touchpoint is a chance to say: we know you had a choice, and we're glad you chose us.

Observational Hospitality
See everything. Say nothing twice.
Read the Guest
  • Guest scanning the room is looking for something. Get there first.
  • Empty glass is not a question. It's a cue. Refill it or ask.
  • Plate pushed to the edge means they're done. Clear it.
  • Two people talking quietly and not looking around want space. Give it.
  • Guest who looks tense or bothered didn't start that way. Find out what happened and fix it.
  • Eye contact from a seated guest means they need you. Respond immediately.
  • Guests still wearing their coats? The room might be cold. Check the temperature before they have to ask.
Read the Room

Before the first guest walks in, the room has to be ready.

  • Floors clean? Every corner, not just the aisle.
  • Bathrooms stocked, clean, and smelling right? Check on a schedule, not when someone complains.
  • Tables fully set? Salt, pepper, stocked jelly and sugar, clean table tent, clean floors and seats.
  • Menus clean? A sticky menu is a first impression.
  • Music right for the daypart? Energy in the room starts with the room.
Anticipate the Need
  • Shareable order? Bring extra plates before they ask.
  • Multiple dishes incoming? Lay out silverware for everyone before food arrives.
  • Table with a baby or toddler? Offer the high chair before the parent wrestles with it.
  • Guest poured their own coffee? You should have been there first.
  • Toast, bread, or a pastry ordered? Butter and jam on the table, already.
  • Hot sauce or ketchup ordered last time? Have it there when they sit down.
Cleanliness Is Hospitality
  • A clean floor says we care before the food arrives.
  • A dirty bathroom says we stopped caring after you walked in.
  • Every Gardener owns the room. Not just their section.
  • If you see it, fix it. That is the standard.
The Language of Observation
Never say

Is everything okay?

Say this instead

Say this

I noticed your glass was getting low.

Say this

I wanted to bring extra plates in case you wanted to share.

Say this

The guest feels seen before they ask for anything. That's the whole standard.

Sell the Brand
Lead with the name. Explain from there.
Why This Matters

Every item on this menu has a name for a reason. That name is the brand. When a Gardener describes a Sunrise Squeeze as "flavored lemonade" or calls Chit Chats "donut holes," the brand disappears. Lead with the name every time. Then explain it.

The Rule
  • Always say the name first. Never a category.
  • Know two details about every item you sell. Name them.
  • If a guest asks what something is, describe it like you love it. Because you should.
  • Generic descriptions make generic restaurants. We are not generic.

The name is the brand. Say it like it means something. Because it does.

Control What We Can
Managers own this page. Every dollar we save is a dollar we don't have to sell.
Counts

A count is the only way to know what we actually used. Guessing is how cash walks out the back door.

  • Count the daily list every day at the same time: proteins, eggs, champagne, and fresh juice.
  • Full inventory once a week. Same day, same time, same manager whenever possible.
  • Count in real units. Never "about half a case."
  • A count that doesn't match usage gets an answer within 24 hours, not a shrug.
  • Same miss at two restaurants is a system fix. Bring it to Chef Wes and Mike.
Waste

If it isn't logged, it didn't happen, and we can't fix it.

  • Log every wasted item when it happens, with a reason: overprep, expired, dropped, remake, or comp.
  • Every remake gets logged with the reason. Remakes show us where training is missing.
  • Review the log at the end of every shift. Your top three waste items set tomorrow's prep.
  • Prep to par by day of week. Not by feel, and not by what we did last Saturday.
  • Label, date, and rotate. First in, first out, every time.
Orders
  • Order to parTwo Sysco trucks a week. Monday and Friday, 8am cutoffs.
  • Trade before you buyCheck on hand and call a sister restaurant first. East Beltline and Standale carry extra to trade with Bridge St and Breton.
  • Check in every deliveryCount it, weigh it, temp it against the invoice. Get credit for shorts before the driver leaves.
  • ShortagesEscalate to Chef Wes, Mike, Erica, and Shelby.

We can't control the weather or the traffic. We can control every count, every waste log, and every order.
